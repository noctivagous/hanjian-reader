1. All men are born with the same good nature, 
but as they grow up, they acquire different habits, 
so they become widely different in their characters.

2. If a man has not been properly taught, his nature will gradually become bad. The most important thing in education is the close cooperation between teacher and pupil.

3. The mother of Mencius carefully chose her neighbours wherever she stayed. 
On one occasion, when her son refused to study, she cut the cloth on her 
loom before his eyes.

4. Old Tou Yen Shan taught his five sons by a good method, and all of them became famous men.

5. To rear and feed children without giving them an education is the father's fault. If teachers are not strict in their teaching, they are idle teachers.

6. It is wrong if children do not study. If they do not study during their younger days, what can they do when they are older?

7. Unpolished gems cannot form ornaments. If a man is untaught, he can never know his duties to his neighbours.

8. In their youth, children should be closely attached to tutors and friends in order to learn the rites and observances.

9. A child should know how to obey his parents. When Hsiang was nine years old, he could warm the bed for his parents to sleep.

10. Children must respect their elders. When Jung was four years old, he could give the bigger pear to his elder brother.

11. First comes duty to parents and superiors. Study follows next. Thus, one will know the laws of numbers and understand written texts.

12. In numbers, we count from units to tens, tens to hundreds, hundreds to thousands, and thousands to ten thousands.

13. The three mighty powers of nature are Heaven, Earth, and Man.

14. The three great celestial lights are the Sun, the Moon, and the Stars.

15. The three binding principles are these: Prince and minister must treat each other fairly; father and son must love each other; husband and wife must be agreeable to each other.

16. The four seasons are spring, summer, autumn, and winter. They succeed one another without fail.

17. North, south, east, and west are called the four directions, which branch off from a central point.

18. Water, fire, wood, metal, and earth are the five elements from which all things are classified.

19. Goodwill, justice, decorum, wisdom, and truth are the five principal virtues. They should not be misunderstood or violated.

20. Paddy, millet, pulse, wheat, fine rice, and panicled millet are the six important grains consumed by mankind.

21. Horses, oxen, sheep, fowls, dogs, and pigs are the six animals reared and domesticated by mankind.

22. Joy, anger, grief, fear, love, hatred, and desire are the seven human passions experienced by all.

23. The eight distinctive musical sounds can be produced from the gourd, clay, leather, wood, stone, metal, strings, and bamboo.

24. Great-great-grandfather, great-grandfather, grandfather, father, oneself, son, grandson, great-grandson, and great-great-grandson are the nine generations in the order of family relationships.

25. The ten moral duties everyone must observe are these: father and son must love each other kindly; husband and wife must treat each other agreeably; elder brothers must be considerate, and younger brothers respectful; superiors and youngsters must treat one another according to their ranks; friends must deal with one another sincerely; the sovereign must esteem his courtiers, and the courtiers must be loyal to their sovereign.

26. In teaching the young, one must explain the full meanings of words, explain idiomatic expressions, and teach them how to distinguish between commas and full stops.

27. Scholars of Chinese should first study the Learning of the Young, which teaches children the art of sprinkling water, sweeping, and behaving properly. It also teaches the six accomplishments: rites, music, archery, driving, writing, and mathematics. After this, they may study the Four Books.

28. The Analects of Confucius contains twenty chapters of the Sage's sayings, recorded by his disciples.

29. The Works of Mencius contains seven chapters explaining humanity and righteousness and discussing virtuous conduct and good principles.

30. The Doctrine of the Golden Mean was written by K'ung Chi, the grandson of Confucius. The word Chung means impartial principles, and Yung means fixed principles.

31. The Great Learning was written by the philosopher Tseng, a disciple of Confucius. It teaches us how to improve our morals and regulate our family life, so that our state may be properly governed and our country made tranquil and happy.

32. After thoroughly learning the Filial Code and the Four Books, students may then study the Six Classics.

33. The Six Classics are the Book of Odes, the Records, the Book of Changes, the Book of Rites, the Rites of Chou, and the Spring and Autumn Annals. Every Chinese scholar should study them.

34. The Book of Changes includes the Lien Shan of the Hsia dynasty, the Kui Ts'ang of the Shang dynasty, and the Chou Ih of the Chou dynasty. These are three theories concerning the changes in nature.

35. The Records contains the Royal and Ministerial Canons, the Instructions, the Injunctions, the Solemn Vows, and the Commands, which are its remarkable writings.

36. The Duke of Chou wrote the Rites of Chou. He appointed the Six Boards to oversee Heaven, Earth, and the four seasons in governing the affairs of the state.

37. Tai Senior and Tai Junior commented on the Book of Rites and retold the sayings of the Sages. The rules of rites and the theory of music are fully discussed in it.

38. The four divisions of the Book of Odes—the Ballads, the Eulogies of the Emperor, the Eulogies of Feudal Princes, and the Sacrificial Songs—should be read by every scholar.

39. The Spring and Autumn Annals was compiled by Confucius. He regarded it as his immortal work and said that through it people would know him. It records historical events, praises virtue, and condemns vice. It was written in an attempt to revive ancient customs, which were declining as emperors gradually lost power to feudal princes.

40. The critics Kung Yang, Tsuo Shih, and Ku Liang wrote three different commentaries on the Spring and Autumn Annals. These commentaries were called the San Chuan.

41. After mastering the Classics, study the books written by the great philosophers. As these books are plentiful, students should choose the important passages and commit them to memory.

42. The Five Philosophers were Hsiin-tzu, Yang-tzu, Wen Chung-tzu, Lao-tzu, and Chuang-tzu.

43. Having studied the Classics and the books written by the great philosophers, study the history of the successive dynasties. Carefully trace their beginnings and endings, and understand the rise and fall of each age.

44. Emperor Fu-hsi, Emperor Shen-nung, and Emperor Huang-ti were the Three Emperors of High Antiquity.

45. Emperor Yao and Emperor Shun were two celebrated emperors whose reigns were known in history as the Golden Age. Both abdicated their thrones in favour of worthier men.

46. King Yu of the Hsia dynasty, King Tang of the Shang dynasty, and King Wen and King Wu of the Chou dynasty were celebrated rulers of antiquity.

47. In the Hsia dynasty, thrones became hereditary, and this continued for four centuries before the dynasty was replaced.

48. The last emperor of the Hsia dynasty was conquered by Tang, who founded the Shang dynasty. This dynasty lasted for more than six centuries before it came to an end.

49. Emperor Chou was a tyrant. He was killed by Wu, who became emperor and founded the Chou dynasty. This dynasty lasted for eight hundred years, the longest of all.

50. After the Chou dynasty came the Ch'in dynasty, which united the empire but did not last long.

51. After conquering Ruler Ch'u, Han became emperor and was known as Emperor Han Kao-tsu. He ruled over the empire of Ch'in and founded the Han dynasty.

52. Emperor Kuang Wu defeated Wang Mang and ruled over the Han Empire. His reign began the Tung Han, or Later Han dynasty.

53. The dynasties of Western Chin and Eastern Chin followed the period of the Three Kingdoms, which were formed by Wei, Shu, and Wu.

54. After these two dynasties came Liu Sung, Chi, Liang, and Ch'en.

55. Yuan Wei, the Northern dynasty, was divided into Eastern Wei and Western Wei. These were followed by Northern Chi and Northern Chou.

56. The empire remained united until the Sui dynasty. But this dynasty did not last more than two generations, and the empire was soon in disorder again.

57. After the Sui came the T'ang dynasty, which brought peace and order to the empire.

58. The T'ang dynasty was followed by the Five Dynasties: Later Liang, Later T'ang, Later Chin, Later Han, and Later Chou.

59. The Sung dynasty succeeded the Five Dynasties. Later, the Southern Sung, Yuan, Ming, and Ch'ing dynasties followed.

60. The history of the successive dynasties should be studied carefully, so that students may understand the rise and fall of rulers and kingdoms.

61. The ancient sages and worthies were diligent in their studies, and their examples should inspire later generations.

62. Sages of ancient times loved learning very much. In his quest for knowledge, Confucius did not hesitate to learn from Hsiang T'uo.

63. Despite his heavy duties, Mr. Chao, a Cabinet Minister of the Sung dynasty, loved to study the Confucian Analects whenever time permitted him.

64. Having no books to write on, Lu Wen-shu wove rushes into mats and used them as writing materials. Kung Sun-hung studied diligently despite his poverty.

65. There were two scholars who, afraid of falling asleep over their studies, devised painful methods to keep themselves awake. One was Sun Ching, who tied his hair to a beam; the other was Su Ch'in, who pricked himself with an awl.

66. Che Yin studied by the light of fireflies, while Sun Kang read by the light reflected from snow. Both persevered despite lacking lamps.

67. Chu Mai-ch'en studied while carrying firewood, and Li Mi hung his book from his cow's horn so that he could read as he travelled.

68. When one is young, one should take one's studies seriously, for it may be too late to learn when one is older. Su Lao-ch'uan realised this and worked hard in his later years.

69. Liang Hao passed the oral examinations in the Imperial Court, where the emperor himself was the examiner. He achieved success at an advanced age, proving that perseverance brings results.

70. Ying of the Northern Ch'i dynasty could recite the Odes at the age of eight, and Pi of the T'ang dynasty could compose unusual verses at the age of seven. They were talented and admired by everyone. Young people should follow their examples.

71. There were two brilliant girls whose examples are worth following. Ts'ai Wen Chi could distinguish the strings of the lute, while Hsieh Taowen could compose poetry.

72. Even at the age of seven, little Liu Yen of the T'ang dynasty was recognised by the emperor as an exceptionally talented boy. Later, he was appointed to high office.

73. Dogs keep watch at night, and cocks announce the dawn. If children do not learn, what do they expect to become when they grow up? Silkworms spin silk, and bees make honey. If people fail to learn, they are inferior to these creatures.

74. Learn diligently while you are young. Do not waste your time, for youth passes quickly and opportunities may not return.

75. The young should learn from the examples of the ancient sages and worthies, respect their teachers and parents, and cultivate good conduct.

76. Diligence brings merit. Time is precious, so always be prepared and strive hard to achieve these precepts.
